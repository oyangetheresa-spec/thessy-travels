---
title: "The Deposit Mistake Every New Host Makes "
date: 2026-10-07
description: "What happens when you hold a unit for a guest who hasn't paid a deposit."
---

This is a mistake I've made more times than I'd like to admit, and it usually starts the same way: a guest who seems genuine, but isn't ready to commit money yet.

Sometimes a guest books days or months ahead, and asking for a deposit that early feels like overkill, they're often not fully sure of their own plans yet either. So you hold the unit in good faith. The risk is what happens if a paying guest shows up while you're waiting.

That happened to me on a Saturday, a peak day. A guest called wanting to see both units before booking, and asked me to hold them until 3pm. While I waited, another guest called, ready to pay immediately. I'd already committed to the first guest and arranged a meetup with my caretaker, so I let the paying guest go.

That time it worked out, the first guest showed up and paid. But it doesn't always go that way. I've waited on guests who never came, and lost a paying client in the process for someone who never existed in the first place.

### Why it keeps happening anyway

The honest answer is temptation. A guest offering a week-long stay sounds like exactly the booking that solves your month, enough to cover bills in one go. So you reserve, even without a deposit, because the upside feels worth the risk.

It's a gamble, plain and simple. Sometimes it pays off. Sometimes it costs you a guest who was ready to pay right now.

### The strict-host trade-off

I've seen hosts solve this by refusing to reserve anything without a deposit, no exceptions. It's the safer policy. But my hesitation has always been the same: what about the guests who are genuinely good for their word, and you lose them over a policy that assumes the worst of everyone?

### What about booking apps?

I used to think apps like Airbnb solved this entirely, a guest checking in months from now has already paid Airbnb in full, so there's nothing to chase. But the more I think about it, the less that holds up.

A guest who refuses to pay a direct-booking deposit and a guest who abandons their Airbnb search because they don't want to pay upfront are the same person, reacting to the same discomfort in two different places. Airbnb didn't remove the gamble, it just moved it earlier, to the moment someone decides whether to even start the booking. And since most of us aren't filling every unit through the apps alone, that version of "the apps have a solution and we don't" doesn't really hold either.

So I've landed somewhere simple: there isn't a fix. It's a gamble no matter which side of the booking you're standing on.

Have you ever reserved without a deposit and had it backfire — or pay off? I'd be curious how other hosts draw this line.
